A

The paper

"Conformalized Interactive Imitation Learning: Handling Expert Shift & Intermittent Feedback"

Michelle Zhao, Reid Simmons, Henny Admoni, Aaditya Ramdas, Andrea Bajcsy. ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.08852.

A robot learns a task by copying an expert (a person who shows and corrects it). Two things make this hard in real life:

  • The expert changes their mind. Yesterday they wanted the cup on the left shelf; today, on the right. The paper calls this expert shift.
  • The expert does not correct every step. They step in only now and then. The paper calls this intermittent feedback.

ConformalDAgger gives the robot an honest error margin around each action, keeps that margin calibrated using only the occasional corrections, and asks for help when the margin grows.

Why rebuild it?

  • The authors have not released code ("coming soon" on the project page).
  • It is Dr Zhao's own first-author work, and the core of her lab's questions: how does a robot know it is wrong, and when should it ask?
  • It is small: a simulated cup-moving task and a small neural network. It runs on a laptop in minutes.
  • It leaves room for my own idea: people's reactions as extra, cheap feedback (Step 5).

B

The idea in one picture

Act, measure how unsure you are, ask when unsure, learn from the answer.

One sentence: if the expert's real actions keep falling outside the robot's margin, the margin grows, the robot asks more, gets more corrections, and relearns the new behaviour. When it is right again, the margin shrinks and it stops bothering the expert.
