2

Feedback rounds: greedy vs sampling

Sampling helps when the model gets stuck repeating the same wrong plan.

Total solved (of 200) after each feedback round

With greedy decoding the model always picks its most likely next word, so given the same feedback it often writes the same wrong plan again: 59 of the round-3 answers were exact repeats. Sampling (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95) in rounds 2 and 3 lets it try something different. Rounds 0 and 1 are identical in both runs. This is one run with one random seed, so a difference of a few problems is within noise.

What goes wrong

Almost every failure is a false precondition: the model loses track of the state.

Goal not reached (Blocksworld / Mystery)—10 / 2

Solved by problem size (Phase 2, greedy, 50 problems per size)

Bigger problems are much harder: half of the 3-block problems are solved after feedback, but only 7 of 50 with 6 blocks. A 6-block plan means carrying a longer list of facts through more steps, and one wrong fact early on breaks everything after it. This is exactly the error the checker can point to, which is why feedback helps at every size.

The rebuild confirms the paper's two main claims on a much smaller model: checker-written reasoning examples make a model that couldn't plan at all produce valid plans, and training with checker feedback teaches it to repair its own plans. While rebuilding it I also found points in the paper that can't be reproduced as written, such as the state-difference loss (not differentiable) and fine-tuning GPT-4 with custom losses (not possible through its API).

5

Limits of this rebuild

  • One run, one seed. Differences of a few problems (for example 45% vs 47%) are within noise. More seeds are needed before strong claims.
  • Small model, LoRA. The paper's full fine-tuning of an 8B model would likely score higher. LoRA was chosen to fit an 8 GB GPU, and the two weren't compared directly.
  • Own problems, not PlanBench files. The domains and the Mystery renaming match PlanBench, but the problems come from my own generator, so the numbers aren't directly comparable with published PlanBench scores.
  • Only two domains. Logistics, the paper's third domain, is not done yet.
  • Practical losses. Phase 2 uses ordinary token loss with an extra plan-only copy, not the paper's state-difference loss.
  • Long answers cut. A few of the longest 6-block training examples were cut at 3,072 tokens.

6

Next steps

  • Best of k: let the model write several plans per round and let the checker pick a valid one.
  • More seeds and confidence intervals for every number.
  • More Mystery data and several updates per Phase 2 iteration.
  • A larger model (7–8B) on a desktop GPU, to compare with the paper directly.
  • Logistics, and then robot tasks: plans for a robot in a simulated office, checked before the robot moves.
